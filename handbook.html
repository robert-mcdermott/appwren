The program handbook

This guide is for the portfolio team: the people who run an application portfolio program with AppWren, from the first decisions to the yearly rhythm of reviews. The other guides say what each screen does. This one says what to do with them, in what order, and why.

A portfolio program answers three questions about every application the organization runs, and keeps the answers true: what do we run, who answers for it, and what should we do with it? AppWren keeps the record, works out a recommendation from it, and holds the decision people take. The program is the people and the habits that keep all three current.

1. Decide before you start#

A few decisions shape everything after them. Make them on paper first; each takes minutes in AppWren once made.

DecisionWhy it mattersWhere it lives
What counts as an applicationEverything is counted in applications. A definition people can apply the same way keeps the count meaningfulAdmin → Program, the definition; What counts as an application
The unitsEvery application belongs to one unit, which answers for it. Units are how work is shared out, how scope is set, and how leaders see their partAdmin → Units
Who decidesA decision is taken by a manager, or a leader over the application's unit, and never by the person accountable for it. Decide who those people areAdmin → People, roles
Who keeps recordsStewards keep a unit's applications complete and current. One per unit is enough to beginAdmin → People, roles
The tiersHow critical an application is sets how often its record is confirmed and its assessment renewed, and weighs in its business valueAdmin → Program, lists
What is sensitiveCost, sign-in, and backup are withheld from people without a part in an application by default. Add or remove fieldsAdmin → Program, fields

If your organization is a university, an academic medical center, a nonprofit, a company, or a research institute, begin from that starter program: it makes most of the list-shaped decisions for you, and every one can be changed.

2. The roles, as a program uses them#

RoleIn a running program
MemberEveryone. Reads the catalog and keeps the applications they are named on
Accountable ownerNot a role but a name on each application: the one person who answers for it. Confirms its record and answers its assessment. Every application has exactly one
StewardKeeps a unit's applications complete, chases what is missing, and runs campaigns for the unit
LeaderSees a unit and everything beneath it, decides for it, and runs its portfolio reviews
ArchitectKeeps the shared records (capabilities, technologies, vendors) and weighs scenarios
ManagerRuns the portfolio: decides anywhere, shapes the program, makes leadership reports
AuditorReads everything, including the full history, and changes nothing
AdministratorRuns the installation: people, sign-in, appearance, connectors, tokens, webhooks

Keep the portfolio team small: one or two managers, an architect, and a steward in each unit that has many applications. Leaders take part through reviews.

3. The first ninety days#

Weeks 1 to 2: the inventory#

Bring in the applications you already know from a spreadsheet (Import applications). Do not chase completeness: name, unit, accountable owner, lifecycle stage, and sourcing are enough to begin. Portfolio → Applications and Reports → Data quality show what each record lacks.

Then turn on what other systems already know (Facts from other systems): the identity provider lists applications people sign in to that are not on record (Portfolio → Not on record), and support dates arrive by themselves.

Weeks 3 to 6: the first confirmation campaign#

Start a campaign for one unit, asking each owner to confirm their records by a date (Portfolio → Campaigns). Owners are reminded, stewards and leaders are told what stays late, and the campaign closes each item by itself. A first campaign with one willing unit teaches more than any amount of planning; widen it once it works.

Weeks 6 to 10: assessment#

Ask the owners of tier 1 and tier 2 applications to assess them: nine questions, each with five described levels. With an assessment, an application's scores and recommendation mean something. Reports → Dashboard shows what is recommended across the portfolio, and how confident each recommendation is.

Weeks 10 to 13: the first review and the first decisions#

Hold a portfolio review for one unit (Portfolio → Reviews), with an agenda drawn from what needs deciding: applications whose decision differs from the recommendation, decisions due again, and those with none. Decide in the room. Each decision says what to do (invest, maintain, tolerate, modernize, replace, consolidate, retire, incubate, or assess further), why, by when, and when to look again.

At the end of the quarter, make the first leadership report (Reports → Leadership reports). It is kept as it was made, so next quarter's can be set beside it.

4. The yearly rhythm#

Once running, the program keeps a rhythm. Most of it AppWren runs by itself; people do the deciding.

ContinuouslyValues from other systems arrive; tasks are raised when a contract's notice date nears, a release in use nears its end of support, a grant ends, or an owner leavesAppWrenTasks, Home
ContinuouslyEach owner confirms their records on the cadence their tier sets (by default every six months for tiers 1 and 2, every year for the rest), and renews assessments as they expire (every year for tiers 1 and 2, every eighteen months to two years for the rest)OwnersHome
Every monthA portfolio snapshot keeps every application's scoresAppWrenReports → Trends
Every quarterEach unit's portfolio review decides what is due, and initiatives are updatedLeaders, managersPortfolio → Reviews, Roadmap
Every quarterA leadership report: where the portfolio stood, how it moved, what was decided, retired, and savedManagersReports → Leadership reports
Twice a yearA confirmation campaign across every unit, to catch what slippedStewardsPortfolio → Campaigns
Every yearReview the program itself: the definition, the lists, the questions, the weights and bands, and the cadence. Preview any change to the scoring model before publishing itManagersAdmin → Program
Every yearLook at the shares of the portfolio (the costliest tenth, the least used fifth) and weigh scenarios for the next year's initiativesManagers, architectsReports → Shares of the portfolio, Portfolio → Scenarios

Running a quarterly review well#

  • Draw the agenda a week before and send it to the people who own what is on it. A review of twenty applications is plenty for an hour.
  • Start with mismatches: a decision that no longer agrees with the recommendation is either a decision to revisit or a record to correct. Both are worth the time.
  • Decide or defer, with a date. A decision to tolerate an application for a year is a decision. Leaving it undecided is not.
  • Close the review when it ends. It becomes a record of what was decided, and nothing in it changes after.

From decision to done#

A decision to modernize, replace, consolidate, or retire offers Start an initiative, which puts the change on the roadmap with an owner, dates, and a budget. A request for something new is checked against what already serves the need before anything is bought. A retirement is a plan with a checklist that ends with the application retired and its savings on record. See Planning change.

Before committing to a large change#

Build a scenario first (Portfolio → Scenarios): name the applications that would be retired, consolidated, or replaced, and AppWren works out what it would save, whom it would affect, which interfaces would stop, and which capabilities would be left with nothing. Nothing changes until it becomes an initiative. See Insight.

5. Measures worth watching#

MeasureRead it fromWhat good looks like
Records confirmed on timeReports → Data qualityRising each quarter, then steady above nine in ten
Applications with an accountable ownerReports → Data qualityEvery one. An application nobody answers for is the first thing to fix
Recommendations made with low confidenceReports → DashboardFalling, as assessments and facts from other systems fill in
Decisions that differ from the recommendationReports → Dashboard, MismatchesFew, and each with a reason someone would stand behind
Applications past end of supportReports → DashboardFalling, each with a decision and a date
Savings from retirementsReports → RetirementsRecorded for every retirement, summed by year
How the portfolio movedReports → TrendsRead quarter to quarter, not day to day

Every figure opens the list it counts. A number nobody can open is not a number to manage by.

6. What goes wrong, and what to do#

SignLikely causeWhat to do
Owners ignore remindersToo many at once, or the first contact was a demandStart with one unit, explain why, and have the leader ask, not the portfolio team
Scores nobody believesThin records and old assessmentsLook at the confidence first. Fill in the record and reassess before arguing with the score. Owners can contest a score with evidence
Reviews run longAgendas too big, or decisions reopened every timeSmaller agendas, decisions with review dates a year out, and mismatches first
The inventory grows faster than it is keptEverything counted as an applicationRevisit the definition: a server, a report, or a module is a component of something, not an application
Decisions never happenNobody's jobMake one manager responsible for every review being held, every quarter
